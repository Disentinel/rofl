# ROFL 1.7 — release notes

Since `rofl-nb-1.6.0` (2026-11-07): 12 pull requests, #233–#244. Every line
names the PRs that made it; what an agent must do differently comes first.
The 1.6 notes are kept in docs/releases/1.6.md, and their known gaps stand
except where a line here says one is closed.

## What an agent must do differently

1. **A corpus is settled by levels; the workers are gone** (#244). `rofl ask`
   and `rofl why` over a world whose facts name two or more files and hold
   32 MB or more cut it by file and settle it as UNITS of one append-only
   scroll: the core frozen once and read by all, the files four at a time by
   the rules that cannot leave a file, then groups of eight units that derive
   only what their children make together, up to the world, and the same
   again for each layer of rules above an absence or an aggregate. Nothing in
   the model declares what crosses files: it comes from the rules' keys, and
   `[surface]` is a book like any other. stderr says `volumes: N files settled by levels, F facts in their
   units (T ms)` where 1.6 said `N files on K workers`; a script that read
   the old line must read the new one (docs/cli.md, *A corpus larger than
   one heap*).
2. **An edit of one file costs about the edit** (#234, #244). The units are
   kept with a registry (`<slot>.q<sum>.scroll`); when at most one file in
   eight moved and the rules did not, only the units that asked what moved
   are settled again: `volumes: X of Y files edited; A of U units settled
   again, S of them concluding what they had`. 250 files of vscode, one fact
   edited in one file: 4.2 to 5.2 s, where 1.6 computed the volumes anew
   (18 to 21 s). A file come or gone is still computed anew.
3. **A corpus larger than the heap goes on from disk** (#233, #236, #244).
   Past a quarter of the memory the process may use, the units done are
   cooled to disk and read from there; stderr says how many times and how
   much. 1 000 files of vscode, which 1.6 answered in part on a 15 GB
   machine, answer whole.
4. **`peak:` over a world by levels is one number** (#244): `peak: the heap
   held N MB (M MB resident at most), while the units were settled`. The
   parts are not said: a unit is gone with them when it is done.
5. **A proof stops at a lemma** (#234, #242). `cut[fixpointplan](B)`
   evaluates the rules below the book B first, keeps what they prove as
   lemmas and drops B; the JS model cuts `[ast_oxc]`, so `why` ends at
   `(a lemma, file.js:4)` instead of walking oxc's tree. `rofl why --all`
   unfolds a lemma. A lemma nothing above reads is not kept, and a question
   about what a stage dropped is refused, not answered empty.
6. **The JS model stores fewer rows** (#238, #240, #241). Projections are
   views, the nearest enclosing scope is declared (`nearest`), walks over the
   call graph go through `eff_reaches`, and the `[surface]` mirrors are
   removed. A `nearest` opens at the level where its negated condition is
   whole. A world kept by 1.6 is recomputed: the build that kept it is named
   in it.
7. **A change is said by feature** (#244): `npm run engine:features -- [N]
   [REF]` scans each tree of the engine into the roles and asks which entries
   (a verb, a flag, an op of `rofl serve`, a declaration) a change moved and
   which effects they gained; `models/engine/intent.rofl` sets what a change
   meant, written as facts, against what it did. Rust is read into the same
   roles as JS (`rust/tools/rs_tree`, `models/lang/rust`).
8. **Iterate the engine on `fast`** (#243): `cargo build --profile fast`,
   `npm run test:fast`, `ROFL_PROFILE=fast`; release only for a number that
   is reported.

## What changed since 1.6.0

**The store**

- **#244** The store of a corpus as one scroll (above). One dictionary of
  names for every unit, the registry written last, each fact's fold, an
  overdeletion judged by the firings the store keeps. Measured on the release
  build, 4 cores, the side-effect question over vscode: 250 files 18 to 21 s,
  750 files 60 to 68 s (the workers it replaced, beside it the same hour:
  19.1 and 54.4 s against 18.1 and 56.0 s), the heap 1.8 GB and 3.9 GB at its
  peak. The ledger has the path: `f_levels_reach_the_workers`,
  `f_a_layer_is_made_ready_once_and_the_sieve_is_gone`,
  `f_a_mended_unit_dropped_the_firings_it_wrote_for_other_stretches`,
  `f_a_unit_of_files_alone_holds_four`,
  `f_after_the_units_one_core_did_work_nobody_needed`.
- **#233, #236** Volumes cooled under pressure, a later cooling writing only
  what changed: the mechanism the units now use.
- **#234, #242** Lemma stages (above).

**A change said by feature** (#244, on the same branch as the scroll)

- `rust/tools/rs_tree` (tree-sitter) writes the raw tree of Rust into
  `[ast_rs]`; `models/lang/rust` reads it into the roles of
  `models/lang/js/roles.rofl` (two words beyond them: `destructs[value]`,
  `ends[tree]`), then names, modules and `use`, receivers' types and calls,
  and the flow of values (a copy, or a wrap through about fifty std methods).
  Over the engine: 590 000 nodes; the call graph keeps 98.9% of the edges of
  the old syn scanner's and finds 1 428 more.
- `models/engine/features.rofl`: an entry is a string a dispatcher matches;
  its behaviour is what its arm reaches by calls and by the values it selects
  (variants, booleans, strings). By calls alone the four question verbs
  reached the same 1 757 functions; by value they part, and no flag of the
  command line is left unexplained (13 before).
- `feature-diff.rofl`, `intent.rofl`, `debt.rofl`: two trees and a change's
  lines say what came, went and moved at each entry and the effects it
  gained; set against what was meant, a move never said is a side effect.
  Over 20 PRs it found that #225 gave `resume` of `rofl serve` the running
  of processes and file writes, said neither in its title nor its report.
- `models/product/vocab.rofl`, `forms.rofl`: the words of a software product
  (product, form, platform, breakdown, unit, entry, feature, effect, contract,
  metric, evidence, view, change, work, snapshot, state) and their relations.

**The JS model**

- **#238, #240, #241** Rows not stored: views, graph closure, `nearest`
  (above).

**Docs, CI, examples**

- **#235** The invariants of CI judged before CI runs
  (`models/process/ci/gate.rofl`, `npm run ci:cost`, the first job of
  ci.yml).
- **#237** The guide: a proof stops at a lemma, larger projects answer whole,
  an edit to a large kept world.
- **#239** examples/cognitive-tours: the lookup half of an audit skill as
  rules.
- **#243** CLAUDE.md: iterate on `fast`, measure on release.

## Known gaps

- **Nothing was measured at the reference scale** (11 100 files): this
  release is what that run is to be made with. The largest world here is
  vscode at 750 files. The parts on one core (reading the facts, checking
  the trees whole, writing the units, concluding in the largest groups) grow
  with the world and do not divide by the cores.
- **News of a round kept into a later one lose answers, and no check tells
  it** (`f_stale_news_of_a_round_is_told_by_no_check`): the code clears them;
  the planted fault for it was told by timing only and is out of the table.
- **CI `breaks` is red on main** by four faults older than this release:
  three `ts_*` plants that no longer find their line, and
  `vclosure_reader_seen_kept`, which survives.
- **A file come or gone is computed anew**; so is an edit of more than one
  file in eight.
- **The feature model is the engine's only**: entries are found in a list of
  dispatchers written by hand, a declaration matched by the lexer (`view`) is
  not seen, the flow of values does not tell one call from another, and no
  check world holds it; a tree costs a minute and 3 GB.
- `models/engine/delta-model.rofl` still holds hypotheses about copies laid
  between volumes, a mechanism that is gone.
- 1.6's gaps stand otherwise: the notebook layer's caches by whole texts,
  Windows has no check that runs, `rofl` costs 25–50 ms of Node in front of
  the engine, red as root in cloud containers. `npm run bench` was not run
  for this release.
