# Dogfood log — Lost-in-Conversation slice on Haiku 4.5 (2026-08-28)

The first externally-authored benchmark run, per the verdict in
`2026-08-28-pilot.md`: microsoft/lost_in_conversation (LIC), sharded-GSM8K
task, run on session subagents (no API keys) with the published evaluator
reused verbatim. Registered hypothesis (Vadim): *with the ledger, Haiku
holds coherence longer than its published degradation point.*

## Setup

- **Samples**: the 6 sharded-GSM8K items with the most shards (12, 9, 8, 8,
  8, 8) from LIC's `data/sharded_math.json`.
- **Arms** (3 per sample, 18 conversations, lockstep turn rounds):
  - `sharded` — LIC's degradation condition: one shard per user turn,
    verbatim.
  - `snowball` — LIC's control: each turn restates everything so far, then
    adds the new shard.
  - `ledger` — ours: each turn delivers ONLY a rendered fact ledger
    (`shard(N,"…")` facts, numbered) framed as "the complete set of
    confirmed task statements", closing with *"If the question is
    answerable from the ledger alone, give the final answer."*
- **Model**: claude-haiku-4-5 session subagents, one persistent
  conversation per (sample, arm).
- **Scoring**: LIC's `TaskMath.evaluator_function`, byte-for-byte; answer
  attempts detected by a system-prompt-mandated `ANSWER: <n>` line.
- **Documented deviations from LIC**: verbatim shard delivery instead of
  their LLM user-simulator (their simulator *paraphrases* shards — our
  version is easier); `ANSWER:`-line detection instead of their
  classifier+extractor; 1 run per conversation instead of 8.

## Results

| arm | final correct | premature wrong attempts | ~tokens |
|---|---|---|---|
| sharded | **6/6** | 3 | 180K |
| snowball | **6/6** | 3 | 184K |
| ledger | **6/6** | 6 | 190K |

Full per-turn record: `bench/lic-haiku45-results.json`.

**The published degradation did not manifest.** Claude 3 Haiku (in the LIC
paper) loses ~39% on sharded conversations; Haiku 4.5 solved every sharded
conversation here, so there was no degradation for a ledger to prevent.
Candidate explanations, in honesty order: (a) two model generations of
progress; (b) verbatim shards are materially easier than the simulator's
paraphrases; (c) n=6 is tiny. The hypothesis is **not refuted** — it is
**untested at this difficulty**: the floor moved above the slice.

**The ledger arm was actively worse on the failure mode that did occur.**
Premature wrong attempts doubled (6 vs 3 vs 3), and every extra one traces
to the ledger *rendering*, not the ledger *idea*:

- The closing instruction "if answerable, give the final answer" is an
  answer-forcing prompt: the moment visible arithmetic closes, the model
  closes it (158 answered $6 at turn 4 off pens alone; both other arms
  asked "did he buy anything else?").
- "The complete set of confirmed statements" converts *absence of a fact*
  into *nonexistence of a fact*. At 968 turn 8 the ledger arm **fabricated
  the missing quantity** (assumed hybrid stringing time = the average,
  17.5 → 18.5 min → 229) rather than report it missing — the exact
  unknown-vs-false confusion the engine's own §5.1 discipline exists to
  prevent (*unknown derives, falsehood never*).

The irony is the finding: a ledger that does not carry the epistemic
discipline (open-world marker: "more statements may arrive; a fact absent
from the ledger is unknown, not false") *injects* the closed-world fallacy
it was meant to cure. The corrected rendering is the next experiment.

Also for the record: the ledger arm cost ~5% more tokens than sharded with
no accuracy gain here — the cost side of the hypothesis showed up; the
benefit side needs harder ground.

## What a real run needs (feeds `docs/benchmark-protocol.md`)

1. LIC's own simulator loop (paraphrased shards), not verbatim delivery.
2. The full 220-sample math set × 8 runs, where the published Haiku
   baseline is actually measurable — plus tasks where 4.5-class models
   still degrade (their code/database/actions tasks, tau2-bench).
3. A ledger rendering with the open-world marker and *without* per-turn
   answer forcing, so the memory variable is isolated from the prompt
   pressure variable.
